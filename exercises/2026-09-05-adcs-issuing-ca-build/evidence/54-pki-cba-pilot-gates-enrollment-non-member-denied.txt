# PKI-CBA-Pilot gates enrollment end to end: a non-member is denied

This closes the gap `evidence/52` left open: whether `PKI-CBA-Pilot` alone, without the removed
`Domain Users` ACE, actually gates enrollment on `district.localClientAuthentication`. No
enrollment attempt as a non-member had been made before or after that ACE removal, until this
exercise.

The test reused `jsmith`, the group's only member, rather than a second account. `jsmith`'s login
at CA01's console was already proven working across `evidence/34`, `39`, and `51`. Removing and
restoring one group membership on a known-good identity avoided a password reset on an untested
account, and isolated one variable: the group, not the user.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this proves
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- `jsmith` held only `PKI-CBA-Pilot` and `SG_Share_Site1_RW` before the change; only
  `SG_Share_Site1_RW` after. The removal took, on the AD side.
- The console logon after removal was fresh, not stale: `whoami /groups` in that session carries
  no `PKI-CBA-Pilot` SID. This rules out the confound `evidence/26` found for `tmp-cainstall`'s
  Enterprise Admins removal, where a live session kept a revoked group's SID.
- `certreq -new` succeeds regardless of enrollment rights: it builds the request locally and
  contacts no CA. It is not a test of the template's ACL.
- `certreq -submit`, run under a token that provably does not carry `PKI-CBA-Pilot`, returns
  `Denied by Policy Module, 0x80094012 CERTSRV_E_TEMPLATE_DENIED`. The CA's policy module itself
  refused the request on the caller's identity, not a client-side or network failure.
- Read together with `evidence/34` (a `PKI-CBA-Pilot` member enrolls successfully) and `evidence/52`
  (the broader `Domain Users` grant is gone), `PKI-CBA-Pilot` now gates enrollment on
  `district.localClientAuthentication` end to end, for both the positive and the negative case.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this does not prove
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Only one non-member was tested, using membership toggled off a single known account rather than a
second identity. This rules out a user-specific confound (a different account's own permissions or
profile state) but does not test a second, independently-provisioned non-member. Not tested here:
whether any other principal — a group nesting, a delegated ACE elsewhere, `Enterprise Admins`, a
future addition to the template — could still bypass this gate. The template's full ACL was last
read whole in `evidence/34`; it has not been re-read since the `Domain Users` removal to confirm no
other broad grant exists.
