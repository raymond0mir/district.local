# tmp-cainstall disabled, before and after

Raymond's decision, following a Microsoft best-practice question in session: disable now, retire
by deletion later after a retention window, because `district.local` has AD Recycle Bin disabled
and a delete here would not be reversible. Disabling closes the practical risk immediately — a
disabled account cannot authenticate, so the standing Full Control ACE on
`district.localClientAuthentication` becomes unusable without needing to touch that ACE itself.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this proves
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

- `tmp-cainstall` moved from `Enabled: True` to `Enabled: False`, before and after both read
  independently of the write.
- `DISTRICT\DC01$`, running as SYSTEM over `qm guest exec`, could disable a regular user account.
  This is a different outcome than the same identity's `Set-ADObject` denial on
  `district.localClientAuthentication` in `evidence/39` — `Insufficient access rights to perform
  the operation`. A domain controller's own machine account has different effective rights on a
  standard user object than on a Configuration-partition certificate template object with a
  custom ACL. No blanket rule about `DC01$`'s AD rights can be drawn from either result alone.
- No credential beyond DC01's own machine identity was used. `tmp-cainstall`'s Full Control ACE on
  the certificate template still stands as an object, unused for this action and unaffected by it.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What this does not prove
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Whether `DC01$` could also disable a protected-group member (AdminSDHolder-governed) the same
way. `tmp-cainstall` is not and has never been in a protected group at time of this capture.
Untested.
