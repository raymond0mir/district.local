The lab

Seven guests, one host, and one administrative path that turned out to be the finding

One Dell Latitude 5420 running Proxmox VE carries the whole forest. DC01 is the only domain controller and the PDC emulator, hardened with no WinRM, no RDP and no PowerShell remoting — which is why every administrative action in this lab runs through the QEMU guest agent from the host shell. That constraint was deliberate. What it did to the audit trail was not, and auditing it became one of the strongest exercises here.

Findings

Seven things this lab proved, with the output to back each one

Every claim below is Captured: a named evidence file holds the machine output, with the command, the host, a UTC timestamp and an exit code. Links go straight to that file.

01
Privileged access

The side that authorises the action records nothing. The side that executes it records everything except who asked.

A controlled qm guest exec invocation with a fixed start time produced zero host-side records: no Proxmox task, no pvedaemon journal line, no pveproxy entry, and auditd isn't installed. DC01 recorded the same invocation three separate ways — the qemu-ga Application log carries the complete command line, event 4688 the process and its parent, event 4104 the script block — and attributed all three to S-1-5-18. No correlation key links the two halves, and the two clocks disagree. On the host side, pveum user list returns root@pam alone: no groups, no ACL entries, no API tokens, and no tfa.cfg.

Why it mattersHardening a box doesn't delete its administrative path — it relocates it. Here it relocated to a root shell with no domain account, no per-VM credential and no time limit. Every property a privileged-access broker exists to impose is absent, and the operator reaches every guest rather than one.

02
Detection

A privileged action that gets refused leaves no trace in the audit log at all.

adm-jsmith, holding PIM eligibility but not an active role, was refused a password reset at 2026-09-10T02:30Z. An unfiltered read of every directoryAudits event in the tenant from 02:00Z returns nothing for it. The nearest records are two Validate user authentication events at 02:30:25 and 02:31:48. The platform logged that the token was valid, and did not log that the account was stopped — most likely because the portal evaluates authorisation before issuing any directory write, so nothing ever reached Core Directory to be logged.

Why it mattersAn account can map the boundaries of its own privilege, one blade at a time, and generate no entry in this log. Only what succeeds is recorded. That matters most for exactly the account this lab exists to study: one that has quietly accumulated standing grants nobody reviews.

Evidence: 13-unfiltered-audit-window

03
Governance

PIM's defaults hand you an audit trail, not a control.

An untouched Entra role management policy requires MFA, requires a justification, and caps activation at PT8H — but sets isApprovalRequired false, with no approvers configured. PIM for Groups is weaker again and differently shaped: a newly created group's member activation policy defaults to On activation, require: None — no MFA at all. And "require Azure MFA" is a claim check, not a prompt: if the session already satisfied MFA at sign-in, activation proceeds silently with no step-up.

Why it mattersA tenant that switches PIM on and changes nothing has bought logging, not gating. The two surfaces also disagree in opposite directions — the group policy refuses a permanent eligible assignment the role policy accepts — so an administrator reasoning from one surface to the other will be wrong both ways.

Evidence: 10-activation-policy-defaults 11-approval-required

04
PKI · hybrid identity

Kerberos survived a 6,829-second clock skew. The certificates did not.

Measured against the NTP-synchronised host, DC01 ran 5h 06m ahead of real time and CA01 7h 00m, leaving CA01 6,829 seconds from the domain controller it authenticates against. Kerberos worked normally throughout — the five-minute tolerance is checked against the KDC's own clock, and the KDC was internally consistent. Certificate validity is not: request 9 resolved at a real 2026-09-09T22:20Z carrying a NotBefore of 2026-09-10T05:10Z. Nothing in the domain noticed, because every validator inside it shared the wrong clock. Entra certificate-based authentication, the stated goal for that CA, does not share it.

Why it mattersThe control most likely to break was fine, and the one nobody was watching was quietly issuing certificates no external relying party would accept. Four induced-load tests then separated the cause from the symptom: the loss is concentrated at boot, not an ongoing drift.

Evidence: domain-time-skew/report.md

05
PKI · troubleshooting

A CA that has never issued a certificate is installed, not working.

The issuing CA reported healthy for four sessions — service running, certutil -ping answering, SetupStatus correct — and could not issue anything. The enrollment wizard's error named CA permissions, CA location and CA trust; all three were ruled out correctly, and the conclusion drawn from ruling them out was still wrong. A direct certreq -submit returned the CA's own policy-module verdict with a specific error code: 0x80094812 CERTSRV_E_SUBJECT_EMAIL_REQUIRED. The template, duplicated from a built-in, required a mail attribute no account in the domain had. Fixing it exposed a second defect underneath — the CA's own certificate carried no CRL distribution point, so it could not validate revocation on its own chain.

Why it mattersThe first defect hid the second for four sessions, and the second was already visible in an evidence file a day before it became the active blocker. A wizard returns a list of candidate causes; a direct submit returns a diagnosis. That difference was worth four days.

Evidence: 39-root-cause-template 47-request-5-issues

06
Active Directory

Revoking a privileged group does not revoke the session already holding it.

tmp-cainstall was removed from Enterprise Admins at 2026-09-07T14:22:03Z. A console session that had logged on before the removal created and fully controlled a new certificate template — a PKI trust object — at 14:43Z, 21 minutes later, on a Kerberos ticket that still carried the Enterprise Admins SID. whoami /groups confirmed the SID present in the stale session and absent after a fresh logon.

Why it mattersThis is standard Kerberos behaviour, not a misconfiguration — which is exactly why it applies to every revocation in the domain or the tenant: a PIM deactivation, a manual removal, an incident-response action. Nothing here forces re-authentication on a privileged group change. Closing the gap needs a capped ticket lifetime or a forced sign-out; neither is configured.

Evidence: 26-stale-ticket-retains-revoked-ea

07
The thesis

The lab produced the exact pattern it was built to study, with its own hands.

Two applications now hold tenant-wide delegated consent for all principals, both granted during lab work. Graph Explorer was consented at 14:40:16.62Z to a scope set including Directory.ReadWrite.All, RoleManagement.ReadWrite.Directory, User.EnableDisableAccount.All and Policy.ReadWrite.ConditionalAccess. Both carry ConsentType: AllPrincipals — covering every account in the tenant, not just the administrator who clicked. Separately: adm-jsmith holds two live paths to User Administrator, and a throwaway account created for a single task still holds Full Control on a production certificate template.

Why it mattersA real requirement appeared, the remedy was a standing grant, it took one click, and nothing asked when it should end. That is the permission-sprawl pattern this whole series is about, reproduced by the person studying it, on a tenant of thirteen users. It is in EXPOSURES.md under my own name rather than quietly cleaned up, because the honest version is the useful one.

Methodology

The capture contract

The October 2025 build of this lab was paired with another assistant, and its audit baseline was imported wholesale. Nobody could separate the verified facts in it from the assumed ones. Everything below exists so that can't happen twice.

Captured
A named evidence file holds the machine output that backs the claim.
Recalled
No machine output backs it. A memory, a screenshot and scrollback are each Recalled — a screenshot is a file, and it is still Recalled.
Inherited
The claim comes from the October 2025 build or its imported baseline. These get re-run first; they carry the highest risk.
Confirmed
A ledger row in verified-claims.md with a live evidence path. 223 of them.
Retired
A Confirmed row moved out, with the reason recorded in the row. 30 of them.
  • Never state a Recalled or Inherited claim as fact. Re-run it and capture it, or move it to Open questions.
  • Every capture block leads with three lines: the command verbatim, the host, and the UTC timestamp from date -u. Then machine output and an exit code. Analysis lives elsewhere.
  • Failed attempts and error output are kept. Failure is evidence.
  • One hypothesis per report. Two hypotheses make two reports.
  • Eight fixed report sections, and "Open questions" is never empty by default — if something wasn't proven, it gets named rather than left implied.
  • A wrong published claim is never corrected by silent edit. It is retired on the record, in the ledger and in the report.
  • validate.py gives binary answers on repository integrity: schema, evidence immutability, carryover length. Its exit code is a gate, not a status.
  • A pre-commit credential scan runs before every commit, written the day a literal password reached report prose.

A plausible unverified claim is worse than no claim. It invites an interview question with no answer.

Domain time skew

Measure the clock difference exercise 26 noticed, and see whether it reaches Kerberos or certificates.

DC01 five hours ahead, CA01 seven — a boot-time misconfiguration (a Linux clock source on a Windows box) that sat dormant and harmless for months, until the domain lost its route to an external time source. What I took from it

What eleven days of this actually taught me

  • Hardening composes, and the composition is invisible one decision at a time. Three individually defensible changes — a direct grant removed, a built-in account disabled, a GPO denying Domain Admins interactive logon — locked every account out of the domain controller. No single one of them was the mistake.
  • "Installed" and "working" are different states, and most tooling reports the first one. A CA answered every health check it had while being structurally incapable of issuing a certificate.
  • A product's defaults are a design decision someone else made for your tenant. PIM ships with approval off. A new role-assignable group ships with no MFA on activation. Neither is announced.
  • Detection surfaces exist and nobody reads them. CA01 logged Id 50 three times and DC01 logged Id 134 six times while the domain's clock drifted five hours. Both are the correct warning, in the correct log, with accurate text. Nothing acted on either.
  • An audit trail that cannot name a human is not an audit trail. The record of a PIM expiry names no actor; the record of a host-shell command names S-1-5-18. The one thing a standing grant can never produce is who asked.
  • Verify before you explain. A plausible explanation closes a question, and a closed question stops collecting evidence. That cost me five days once, and it is the reason the capture contract has no convenience exceptions.
